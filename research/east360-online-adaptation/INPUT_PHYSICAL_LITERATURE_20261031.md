# Physical interpretation of four cross-tokamak inputs

Date: 2026-10-31. Bounded literature sidecar for the ongoing input interventions. The audit medians below were supplied in the task; they were **not independently recalculated here**. No data or experiment code were changed or uploaded.

## Bottom line

The four names point to physically meaningful diagnostic families, but the literature does **not** establish that the current J-TEXT and EAST stored columns are numerically commensurate. In particular, a gain or offset selected because it improves prediction or aligns marginal distributions is a **statistical sensitivity/alignment intervention**, not proof of an instrument conversion, a recovered physical law, or a root cause. A physically justified conversion would require independent measurement provenance and calibration.

Shen's PGFE-U does intend to use horizontal and vertical displacement divided by minor radius. This is a real physical nondimensionalization, distinct from z-score normalization. It is **not evidence that the current columns actually implement that formula on both devices**. The `dx_a`/`dy_a` mapping is the project's intended naming interpretation, not a mapping proved by the papers or the missing upstream script.

The four median comparisons reflect the audit's sampled populations and processing. They do not by themselves identify measurement error: dissimilar units or scaling, diagnostic geometry/response, true operating-regime and wall differences, sampling/shot selection, reference offsets, missing values, and combinations thereof remain competing explanations. In particular, do **not** turn a median ratio into a claimed conversion factor or conclude from the gap alone that precisely one device omitted `/a`.

## Physical calibration versus statistical alignment

**Direct evidence.** Shen 2024 §4 states that each device is independently normalized by its feature mean and standard deviation; its cross-tokamak EAST example uses 110 known discharges for normalization. In §5.1, the authors describe normalization and CORAL as aligning features “in terms of data distribution.” CORAL additionally aligns covariance and is not generally a per-feature scalar transform. The [2026 publisher preview](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/412244036_The_role_of_data_quality_and_alignment_in_cross-tokamak_disruption_prediction) says EFD supplies target-machine z-score statistics and explicitly says those statistics were estimated from real EAST shots. Its figure caption says no EAST shots were used for **classifier training**. Those statements are compatible: classifier-training zero-shot is not automatically target-data-free normalization.

The dissertation makes the qualification particularly concrete: [§4.5.1](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/docs/literature/paper_131.txt:2831) says the means and standard deviations in its demonstration were computed from **10 disruptive EAST discharges**, not used to train the classifier. The passages inspected here do not establish whether those shots overlap the reported test cohort; do not infer either overlap or independence. The thesis discusses design parameters or simulations as possible future ways to estimate such statistics, not as proof that its demonstrated constants were derived without EAST data.

**Interpretation.** Device-specific z-scores are affine, but they put observations in units of each selected distribution's standard deviation, not necessarily in a shared physical calibration. Algebraically, when source and target statistics are defined in the same raw feature representation, standardizing the target as `(x_t - μ_t)/σ_t` is equivalent, for a fixed source standardizer, to first mapping it to `x'_t = μ_s + (σ_s/σ_t)(x_t - μ_t)` and then applying the source z-score. This is a statement about the transformation, not a proposed numerical mapping or evidence that the physical states match. Means and standard deviations depend on population and sampling; using target outcomes or future target data to choose them changes the evaluation protocol.

**When can gain/offset be physical?** When separately documented instrumentation, units, offsets, sign conventions and matched physical observables justify it, with calibration checked against an independent reference over the relevant range. For displacement, a known coordinate origin/polarity and known device-specific minor radius are needed before applying or reversing `dr/a` or `dz/a`. For magnetic amplitude, calibrated field units and sensitivity/geometry/field-compensation information are needed; probe-to-mode distance and equilibrium can make a fixed scalar inadequate. For CIII, matching spectral response, view, calibration and plasma/wall conditions matters. An affine transform can be a useful approximation under evidence-backed conditions, but none of the observed median differences identifies those conditions or their constants.

## Implication for the ongoing one-factor interventions

- Keep the source model and preprocessing fixed and describe each changed target input as a **single-channel input perturbation**; record whether it was applied before or after standardization, and compare to the unmodified baseline. A gain/offset in standardized coordinates is not automatically the same intervention as one in raw diagnostic coordinates.
- If the alarm metrics change, the supported conclusion is that this fitted pipeline is sensitive to that channel and intervention under the evaluated distribution. It does **not** prove the old data have a unit bug, that the new values are physically correct, or that the channel caused the disruption. A null result also does not establish calibration.
- Maintain physically meaningful sign/zero/range and missing-value semantics where known; in particular an arbitrary offset can turn an amplitude or radiation signal into an implausible negative value. Do not clip or redefine signs silently, since that changes the intervention.
- If target data are used to choose the transform, label that target calibration/adaptation explicitly. Use a disjoint calibration/validation set or strictly past-only prequential statistics as appropriate to the declared protocol, and reserve held-out outcomes for evaluation. A transform selected on the same target evaluation outcomes is exploratory, not unbiased evidence of deployment gain.
- The most informative next non-GPU evidence is the original PGFE-U producer and per-device diagnostic metadata: raw channel/units, units after every transform, minor radius and coordinate origin/polarity, sensor/channel identities and geometry, calibration/background/compensation, missing-value conventions, and the shot/time populations used for any summary statistics.

## Verification limits and rejected overclaims

- **Supported by the read excerpts:** the locked-mode measurement definition, CIII central-channel designation, thesis PGFE-U displacement normalization, and statistical nature of Shen's feature-standardization procedure.
- **Unresolved:** the actual meaning and units of the four current stored columns, a valid cross-device conversion, exact 2026 PGFE-U per-column methods, and whether the thesis EFD shots overlap its EAST test sample. None can be resolved from the supplied medians.
- **Rejected as unsupported:** “the median ratio is the physical unit conversion”; “one device definitely omitted `/a`”; “CIII is total impurity concentration”; “n=1 amplitude is island width”; “zero classifier-training EAST shots means no EAST data were used anywhere.”
- Four primary journal works were consulted, with access levels specified above; the 2024 thesis and author poster were also read. The above is a source-to-claim check by one assistant, not an independent blind adversarial review. The proposed ScienceDirect optical-diagnostic result was not used: its full page was inaccessible, and it could not establish the exact channels in this dataset.
