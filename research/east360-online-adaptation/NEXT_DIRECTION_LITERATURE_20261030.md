# EAST intra-shot adaptation: bounded primary-literature sidecar

2026-10-30. Scope supplied by the parent: the qualified J-TEXT transformer stays fixed; the first 20 EAST shot labels are available only after their respective shots; the remaining 340 shots are unlabeled for adaptation, intra-shot only. The recently tested scalar arrival-evidence × original-risk-gradient update gave only one borderline rescue, indistinguishable from simple controls. This is context supplied by the parent, not independently re-evaluated here.

## Five papers: what actually changes

1. **Deng et al., “Learning to Generate Gradients for Test-Time Adaptation via Test-Time Training Layers,” AAAI 2025.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16901); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i15.33783). Read §§3.2–3.5, Algorithm 1, and §4.2.
   - For unsupervised gradient `g_t = ∇θ L_TTA`, the actual model update is `θ_{t+1} = θ_t − η MGG(g_t; φ)`. The gradient-memory parameters also change online by minimizing `||GML(φ_K g_t; φ_m) − φ_V g_t||²`; the learned projection parameters are fixed at deployment. The paper's TTA loss combines prediction entropy with source-versus-target feature mean/variance discrepancy, not a disruption loss. Its held-out optimizer training uses 128 unlabeled ImageNet-C validation images; deployment updates the model's normalization parameters.
   - Source check (§3.3): “MGG takes the gradients computed from the unsupervised loss as input and outputs optimized gradients for more stable parameter updates.” Limitation: §3.5 explicitly models **each parameter independently**, sharing the other generator weights; it does not demonstrate joint cross-parameter gradient transport. Image classification and the offline validation set differ from a single causal EAST trajectory.

2. **Goyal et al., “Test Time Adaptation via Conjugate Pseudo-labels,” NeurIPS 2022.** [Full paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/28e9eff897f98372409b40ae1ed3ea4c-Paper-Conference.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-0449). Read §§2–4.3 and Algorithm 1.
   - Their *exploratory* learned loss `m_φ(logits)` drives `θ' = θ − α∇θ m_φ`, while `φ` is trained through that step to reduce a supervised outer loss. The authors ultimately propose a conjugate loss/pseudo-label recipe rather than deploying this meta-learning experiment as their method. For the stated loss family `L(h,y)=f(h)−yᵀh`, their unsupervised objective is `−f*(∇f(h))`; the source classifier is genuinely updated on unlabeled inputs.
   - Source check (§2): “Note that the meta-learning process above assumes access to labels y of test inputs.” Their experiment uses labeled validation corruptions (§3). A learned EAST outer objective therefore needs previously permitted labels; it cannot obtain them from the 340-shot stream. Their approximation assumes appropriate loss structure and a sufficiently good source model; entropy/pseudo-label certainty is not evidence of physical truth.

3. **Chen et al., “Gradient Alignment Improves Test-Time Adaptation for Medical Image Segmentation,” AAAI 2025.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07343); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v39i3.32244). Read Method: “The Objective of GraTa,” Algorithm 1, “Dynamic Learning Rate”; Experiments: “Discussions.”
   - GraTa forms a provisional `θ' = θ − ∇L_ent(θ)`, evaluates weak/strong-augmentation consistency at `θ'`, and applies its consistency gradient from the original `θ`; its learning rate is a function of the cosine between the two gradients. Their first-order explanation is `L_con(θ) − ∇L_con(θ)·∇L_ent(θ)`. This is an actual parameter update, not merely a history-conditioned prediction.
   - Source check (Method): “In this study, we introduce the entropy loss L_ent as the auxiliary objective function to align with the consistency loss L_con.” Limits: the two signals derive from unlabeled images and augmentation; the true-label gradient in their discussion is an evaluation diagnostic, not available for deployment. Their image transforms and segmentation results do not establish valid plasma-signal augmentations or event-risk alignment.

4. **Wang et al., “Decoupled Prototype Learning for Reliable Test-Time Adaptation,” arXiv v2, 2024.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08703); [arXiv record and DOI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08703). Read §III, §IV-A, and §V-B. This is the inspected preprint, not an asserted conference publication.
   - The paper treats classifier weight vectors as class prototypes. Its prototype-centric contrastive loss brings each prototype toward confidently pseudo-labeled positive features and away from other pseudo-classes; an EMA memory of class pseudo-features contributes a second contrastive regularizer. Model parameters are updated from the combined loss during testing; the target is not just a frozen nearest-prototype prediction.
   - Source check (§IV-A): “Specifically, we reduce the distance of each prototype from its positive samples and increase its distance from negative samples.” Limits: pseudo-labels can be systematically wrong, absent classes and small batches matter, and the experiments are image classification. A binary shot outcome does not automatically label every window in that shot.

5. **Fentazi et al., “Training on the Future: A Delay-Aware Audit of Test-Time Adaptation for Time-Series Forecasting,” arXiv v1, 2026.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12232); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12232). Read §§3–4 and §§5.1–5.2. This is a recent preprint; its arXiv DOI was marked pending on the inspected record.
   - A frozen forecaster's adapter really updates from matured diagnostic targets; for an `H`-step *complete* target window, the paper releases it no earlier than `s+H`, and also distinguishes causal partial-prefix updates. Its RLS example updates `w ← w + P x (y−wᵀx)/(λ+xᵀPx)` on matured pairs. The paper reports that rankings and harms depend on dataset and delay; a frozen model and simple correctors are substantive controls, not proof of superiority in EAST.
   - Source check (§3): “We call d=H the minimum causal delay: a window is used as soon as it has been fully observed.” Here “label” is a future **forecast value**, not the future disruption outcome.

## Separate inference for the 23-feature EAST setting

- The five papers do not validate any EAST method. A fixed forward model using a longer history or a residual-conditioned score is **history-conditioned prediction**; it is not within-test parameter adaptation. An explicitly changed 23-vector input adapter that affects *later* scores is adaptation. The original transformer weights, risk head, operating threshold and alarm policy remain unchanged.
- The following are proposals, not replicas: an external multiplicative feature adapter `a ∈ R^23` starts at zero each shot and supplies `x' = exp(a) ⊙ x` to the fixed model. The parent must verify feature order, units, causal preprocessing, availability of matured features, and that the insertion preserves the original pipeline at `a=0`; no causal 23-channel feedback means no such experiment without a justified mask. Neither feature scaling nor prototype attraction is guaranteed physically meaningful.
- Fit all shared sidecars, scaling, prototypes, hyperparameters and stopping rules using only allowed data from the first 20 completed shots, with shot-level cross-validation. Fit the forecaster and prototypes on the training shots of each fold, not on its held-out shots; lock choices before refitting on the 20. Use the existing risk-label/timing contract in any supervised outer loss; never assign a disruption label to every window merely because its shot eventually disrupted. Freeze shared sidecars before shot 21. Thereafter reset `a`, optimizer state, queues and any online memory at every shot; no 340-shot outcomes or cross-shot state enter updates or selection.
- Conservative timing: at time `t`, record the alarm from the current state first; then release only forecasts whose full 25-sample diagnostic target has actually arrived (`s+25 ≤ t`, plus any real sensor latency), update for later times, and store the next forecast. Use only the same shot's history. Never call a future outcome a diagnostic target. Partial-prefix feedback is possible but is **not** the full-horizon experiment below.

## Two executable ablations for the parent

**Shared feedback construction.** On the first 20 completed shots, fit a ridge forecaster `q_ψ` from the last 32 causal, already-preprocessed 23-feature samples to the next 25 × 23 observed feature values; freeze it. At forecast origin `s`, save the history and adapter snapshot `a_s`. Once the full target matures, compute `g_s = ∇_a mean[(q_ψ(H_s^a) − X_{s+1:s+25})²]` at `a=a_s` and apply the resulting **stale** gradient to the current adapter. Use the same valid-channel mask, update times, bounded `a`, optimizer budget and score-before-update ordering in all arms. Skip origins without 32 observed samples; no update occurs before feedback exists.

1. **Conditional multi-direction transport versus scalar.** Set `a ← Π_B(a − η M_φ(c_s) g_s)`, with `M_φ(c)=D+U diag(ρ_φ(c))Vᵀ`, `D` diagonal, `U,V ∈ R^{23×2}`, and `c_s` containing only the matured 23-channel forecast residual and the saved risk score at `s`. Learn `D,U,V,ρ_φ` by unrolling this update on first-20-shot pseudo-episodes and minimizing the *existing* supervised risk loss on later windows of those completed shots; freeze them for the 340-shot stream. This combines the learned-update idea with Goyal's labeled outer objective; it is **not** Deng's parameter-wise optimizer.
   - Compare the frozen adapter, direct forecast-gradient update (`M=I`), constant learned `D+UVᵀ`, conditional `M_φ(c)`, and the parent's existing scalar arrival-evidence × risk-gradient control. Match per-update norm/step count where nonzero, clipping and bounds; report the fraction of zero/skipped updates. A gain only over `M=I`, but not over constant transport or the existing scalar control, does not isolate conditional direction learning.

2. **State-prototype feature alignment versus forecast only.** On first-20-shot histories, cluster shot-balanced 32-step means of the 23 preprocessed features into four fixed unlabeled state prototypes `p_k`. At `s`, select the nearest prototype using only `H_s^{a_s}` and hold that assignment fixed. On maturation compute `g_s^p = ∇_a ||mean(H_s^a) − p_k||²` at `a_s`; update `a ← Π_B[a − η(g_s + λ_p g_s^p)]`. This is a deliberately simplified state-alignment analogue, **not** DPL's classifier-weight or pseudo-class loss, and uses no guessed risk labels.
   - Compare `λ_p=0` with a first-20-selected nonzero `λ_p`, plus a fixed shuffled-prototype assignment control; match update budget. Report nearest-prototype distance, directional cosine with `g_s`, skipped/oversized updates and whether unusual pre-event states are pulled toward common states. If anchors erase a precursor or collapse to a single state, forecast improvement alone is not a success.

**Evaluation boundary for both.** Record feature forecast error, update count/norm/direction and changed risk trajectory online. If the parent has separate authorization for sealed post-run outcomes, compare paired shot-level alarms at the already fixed operating policy, including timely detections, missed events, false alarms and lead-time changes; include the frozen and existing simple controls. Otherwise no event-performance conclusion can be drawn. With 20 labeled development shots and a borderline prior result, quantify uncertainty and treat any rescue/harm as exploratory; do not claim novelty, physical discovery or efficacy.

## Verification and limits

Five primary full texts retained; the equations and section locations above were checked against them. Two initially tempting claims were rejected: Deng's per-parameter memory does not establish cross-parameter mixing, and Goyal's exploratory meta-training is not label-free. One additional candidate was excluded after only its abstract was accessible. No independent-context skeptic/spawn interface was available, so this is a same-context source check rather than the separate adversarial review prescribed by the verification skill. Exa search accounts returned credit-limit errors; discovery used generic web queries and the cited primary records only. No experiment, GPU job, source-data upload or outcome evaluation was performed.
