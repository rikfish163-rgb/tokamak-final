# Paired fast-on/off gap guard: bounded history and design audit

**Scope and verdict.** I searched the accessible experiment implementations, saved protocols, code snapshots, and completed results for the specific combination of (1) a paired fast-on versus fast-off risk-loss contribution, and (2) protection or optimization of that contribution *across persistent EAST between-shot updates of S and O*. I did **not** find a completed experiment implementing that exact combination in this bounded search. This is not a claim that it is absent everywhere in the project, or that the proposed idea is scientifically novel. Several completed experiments already used genuinely paired on/off risk objectives or screens, but at a different training stage, parameter scope, comparison unit, or reference state. The five closest are below.

I did not inspect the current all-57 census outputs. The D/source contribution deterioration for update 65067 is taken from the request, not independently re-measured here. The [original 65067 receipt](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/slow_fast_attribution_v1/updates/0222_65067.json) independently establishes that the `both` proposal was accepted and its **fast-on absolute** historical-S, historical-D and source-event guard losses all fell. It does not contain the corresponding fast-off losses, so it alone cannot verify the reported contribution changes.

For this audit, write `G_g(theta) = L_g(on; theta) - L_g(off; theta)` on the *same* fixed, authorized replay/source examples and the same parent parameters `theta = (S,O)`. A candidate gap guard would compare `G_g(theta_candidate)` with `G_g(theta_before)` for each available group; smaller is better. This is different from merely checking `L_g(on; theta_candidate) <= L_g(on; theta_before)`.

## Five closest completed precedents

1. **Source FFN subspace `risk_protect`: true paired on/off risk optimization, but not an S/O update guard.** The executed [archived implementation](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/experiments/risk_aligned_online/runs/ffn_subspace_protected256_20261009/code_snapshot.zip) evaluates the same source query with zero fast state and with a signal-updated fast state, and trains the source basis with `L_after + relu(L_after - stopgrad(L_before))`. Its `risk` arm is explicitly mapped to `risk_protect` by the archived trainer. Only the learned rank-8 FFN basis is persistently trained, with the original classifier fixed; the temporary online step is signal-driven. It is therefore an actual paired contribution **penalty**, not a hard guarantee, and not a per-group comparison of the fast contribution before versus after EAST S/O training. With the fast-off parent fixed, the reference is constant with respect to the basis: the hinge reweights harmful episodes rather than protecting a moving slow reference. The [completed 256-step protocol](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/experiments/risk_aligned_online/runs/ffn_subspace_protected256_20261009/protocol.json) and [validation](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/experiments/risk_aligned_online/runs/ffn_subspace_protected256_20261009/validation.json) record the protected model under `risk`: source class-0 mean loss increased from 0.654330 to 0.655871 despite the penalty. In the separate [fixed-API source VAL160 full-shot result](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/experiments/risk_aligned_online/runs/ffn_subspace_val160_fixed_api_20261009/result.json), static was 73 TP/6 FP and protected was 73/7, each out of 80 D/80 S. These are development source results, not an EAST trial. The current [orientation code](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/ffn_subspace.py:115) is **not byte-identical** to the executed file; use the snapshot for the execution claim.

2. **Cold20 Q/K protected policy: a hard paired risk-gap screen, but on a fixed parent, not across S/O updates.** The archived and hash-matched [acceptance helper](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/attention_protected_20261029/learning.py:30) requires every fitted shot's on-minus-off threshold-relative operational loss to be at most `1e-6`, and the disruptive-class mean to improve by more than `1e-6`. [The fitting code](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/attention_protected_20261029/train.py:25) backtracks a Q/K policy on ended cold20 shots; S and the underlying parent are frozen. The risk comparison is paired by shot but uses separately selected whole-shot eligible peak logits, not necessarily the same endpoint. It protects an action against fast-off on fit shots; it does **not** compare that action's contribution across an S/O proposal. [Cold20 completion](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/attention_protected_cold20_v1/complete.json) records a nonzero 0.25 step and policy-only crossfit 3 improved/11 worsened/6 tied; the cold parent had already used all 20 shots, including 17 D/3 S. The [completed EAST360](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/attention_protected_east360_v1/complete.json) records cold, protected, random, reverse and constant controls all at 125/32 out of 180 D/180 S, with no classification flips relative to cold. Its own screen was false. The helper is present and hash-matched in the snapshot, but the fitting entrypoint itself is not in that snapshot; the current entrypoint is supporting code, not a byte-verified executed copy.

3. **Cold20 Q/K weights and basis: paired on/off risk-gap optimization with documented zero-step collapse.** [The shared objective](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/attention_weights_20261029/learning.py:27) computes the threshold-relative risk loss of the fast-on eligible peak minus that of a frozen-parent fast-off peak, adding a second penalty for positive differences, macro-averaged over D/S. The [basis fit](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/attention_basis_cold20_v1/complete.json) selected **step 0**, with 20/20 crossfit ties. Even the [staged fit](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/attention_basis_cold20_staged_v2/complete.json) returned step 0, and its [360 stream](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/attention_basis_east360_staged_v2/complete.json) records zero updates in all four candidate/control arms. This is direct historical evidence that a related relative objective can choose no action; it does not prove the proposed S/O guard must do so. These crossfits also inherit the all-20 cold-parent exposure.

[Source completion](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/experiments/risk_aligned_online/runs/reference_gradient_source_service_20261028/complete.json) records matched joint-reference and stopped-reference arms. 5. **Cross-channel source promotion screen: true per-class on/off risk test, not an online S/O guard.** The executed and hash-matched [screen](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/cross_channel_train.py:56) requires paired mean query risk loss to improve and neither endpoint-label class to worsen after the fast step. It is a *source held-out episode promotion screen* after decoder fitting, not a before/after comparison of the same persistent EAST S/O candidate. The inner step happens to use an auxiliary reconstruction-loss acceptance check; the cited promotion criterion is a separate risk comparison, not that auxiliary guard. The [completed source artifact](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison/experiments/risk_aligned_online/runs/cross_channel_source_20261021/completed.json) records all four screens failing on 128 episodes. For example, risk-embedding overall mean fell from 1.0354 to 0.9595 while class-0 loss rose from 0.0408 to 0.2081. This is a concrete precedent for avoiding aggregate compensation, not evidence of a persistent gap guard or whole-shot EAST gain.

## Current lifecycle context and what the guard would mean

The hash-matched [lifecycle learning code](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/lifecycle_stream_20261025/learning.py:105) computes the existing guard separately for available historical S, historical D and source events. For `both`, it measures **fast-on** absolute risk loss before and after two Adam steps and rejects if any available group increases by more than `1e-7`. The cold fit mixes on/off losses, but that does not make the subsequent `both` guard a gap guard. Both S and O are updated; the already-computed causal delta is detached. The [runner](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/experiments/risk_aligned_online/lifecycle_stream_20261025/run.py:183) seals predictions before outcome reveal and calls the update with the prior replay before appending the just-ended example. The source-event bank is reused in training and guarding, so the guard is not an independent test.

The [completed attribution replay](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/slow_fast_attribution_v1/completed.json) is diagnostic only: it reports 1,800 exact original curves, 680 exact update receipts and exact final inter/both states, not a newly trained policy. Its [independent audit](/media/hetaisheng/044A81D94A81C83E/Fuison-intra-trajectory-20261028/probe_outputs/slow_fast_attribution_v1/independent_audit.json) reports that, at the same `both` state, fast-on added false alarms for 64562, 83376 and 94402 relative to fast-off. It also says that verifier did not re-execute training. Retrospective use of a later case input to locate a parameter transition is explanatory, not information available to an online guard.

The distinction is algebraic, not semantic. For example, on/off losses `(0.40, 0.50) -> (0.39, 0.45)` both improve, but `G` worsens from `-0.10` to `-0.06`. Conversely, `(0.40, 0.50) -> (0.42, 0.70)` improves `G` to `-0.28` while the deployed fast-on loss worsens. A gap guard alone is therefore neither an absolute-risk guard nor an alarm guarantee.

## Is a simple gap guard still trainable?

**Yes in principle, but it is a filter, not by itself an objective, and nontrivial retained learning is unproven.** Adding `G_after <= G_before + epsilon` to the existing absolute guard leaves the existing supervised/replay/source loss and its S/O gradients in place; it can accept a nonzero update if one satisfies all constraints. For example, abstract losses `(0.40, 0.50) -> (0.37, 0.48)` improve both absolute losses and the gap. That numerical example is not evidence such a candidate exists in this model. A hard accept/reject rule supplies no gradient to fix a failing candidate unless an additional, explicitly designed optimization or search procedure is used.

The trivial feasible strategy is to reject every proposal and retain the old S/O; accepted-update rate and retained parameter changes must therefore be measured. Zeroing fast is another possible degeneracy, but **not always feasible**: if `O=0` makes on and off exactly equal, `G_new=0`. It passes a simple no-worsening gap check when the old gap is nonnegative (up to tolerance), but violates it for an old sufficiently negative gap. It can still destroy useful action on examples or groups not represented by the protected averages. Conversely, an unchanged or nonzero `||O||` does not prove useful fast behavior: the realized `O delta`, logit effect, and paired losses can be nearly zero or cancel across examples. The earlier step-zero Q/K result shows why this is an empirical check rather than an assumption.

If someone instead optimizes a gap penalty, its reference needs special care. For the current shared S, `grad_S G = grad_S L_on - grad_S L_off`; `L_off` generally changes with S. Optimizing a joint difference can improve the ratio by degrading the off reference. Detaching the off branch changes that gradient and does not prevent the shared S from moving the off prediction on the next evaluation. Preserving the existing on-loss guard and monitoring or separately constraining off-loss changes are possible protections, with an explicit feasibility/acceptance tradeoff; neither should be silently claimed to prove future safety.

## Highest-priority controls for a forward rerun

1. **Prespecify the intervention.** Start from the same qualified fixed J-TEXT checkpoint, cold S/O state, mapping, threshold, optimizer recipe, random seeds and stream order. Compare at least existing absolute guard, absolute-plus-gap guard, and no-persistent-update/cold control. Include independently evolved S-only/O-frozen and O-only/S-frozen controls if the budget permits; the existing `inter` branch is not automatically a clean S-only ablation of `both` because its mode, guard and resulting history differ. An acceptance-matched or budget-matched rejection control can help distinguish selective protection from merely updating less. Match a candidate's pre-state and replay/source examples to measure a guard's immediate effect; after the first divergent acceptance, run each policy forward on its own genuine state history. Do not attribute full-stream differences to a single isolated acceptance.
2. **Audit every proposal, not only accepted ones or selected cases.** For each available group, record the four losses `L_on_before`, `L_off_before`, `L_on_after`, `L_off_after`, both gaps `G_before` and `G_after`, and both guard decisions, along with signed component differences, tolerances, actual retained S/O norms and rejection reasons. Use identical endpoint IDs, labels, validity masks, loss weighting and causal fast delta within the four-way comparison. Record historical S/D counts and source-event composition; if a historical class is absent, mark it absent rather than inventing a zero or peeking at a future shot. Existing target policy and source policy differ, and mean group losses can hide harmful individual shots or event families: report their distributions as diagnostics, without retroactively changing the acceptance rule.
3. **Make fast attribution conditional on the same persistent state.** For each forward arm, emit a fast-off shadow using the same S/O, input, causal schedule and current-shot prefix as fast-on. Compare `both` with its own `both_shadow`, and `intra` with its own cold; `both` versus `inter` mixes distinct learning histories. Record realized `||O delta||`, fast-on/off logit and probability differences, nonzero-action fraction, update opportunities, and earliest affected prediction, including predictions already sealed before fast can act. Use matched-norm reverse/constant or otherwise time-disordered feedback as declared controls; count zero directions explicitly. Compare the action and its functional effect, not merely parameter norms or auxiliary forecast loss.
4. **Separate slow, output and interaction effects.** For predeclared diagnostic versions, evaluate S-before/O-before, S-after/O-before, S-before/O-after and S-after/O-after with both fast settings on identical authorized inputs. Nonlinearity means the S-by-O interaction need not be additive. A cold-O or zero-fast control helps detect suppression, but cannot by itself identify why a future false alarm occurred. Case-specific later inputs are post-hoc analysis only and must never enter the online update/guard.
5. **Preserve causality and fair sampling.** Form the gap guard only from already revealed prior replay plus the fixed authorized source bank, as requested; the just-ended current shot may enter the existing training objective only after sealing/reveal and must not be silently added to the gap guard. Never use an unsealed current outcome or future outcomes. Preserve the original endpoint selection rule and log each arm's selected endpoints; if scores make selections diverge, report that as part of the total policy effect or prespecify an online reference selector. Keep replay membership, masks, missing-class behavior, optimizer/RNG state and rejection rollback auditable. Source replay is not independent held-out validation.
6. **Judge alarms as well as surrogate losses.** Keep threshold and strict lead-time rule fixed. Report complete full360 including warmup, suffix340, D/S denominators, TP/FP, first-alarm lead distribution including very early alarms, and paired rescued/lost D and added/removed S false alarms. Report acceptance and nonzero-action rates beside these outcomes and the per-group losses. The existing stream and source were development-exposed; require a separately held-out future cohort/order and additional seeds for a generalization claim. A smaller mean gap, better forecast, or identical aggregate TP/FP is not proof of a useful or physically correct adaptation.
