## Bottom line

The most useful new evidence is **VITA's learned reconstruction target and sequential fast-state update**: its supervised outer objective differentiates through online updates, and its within-trajectory memory is separately compared with reset alternatives. The closest new contrast is **Highlight-TTA's meta-auxiliary ablation**, but its full-video access is not a causal shot stream. **Wu et al.** explicitly train over a stream of unlabeled inner updates, but update the shared image encoder. **Finn et al.** provide an inter-task replay template, but their few-shot implementation uses current-task labels and is not an unlabeled intra-shot algorithm. None validates a frozen, already-qualified J-TEXT Transformer/risk head on EAST.

The EAST physics papers add a concrete warning: a period of ordinary H-mode evolution can include rising density and radiation alongside falling core temperatures, while a different density-ramped EAST discharge can end in collapse. These are different experiments, not paired counterfactuals. Neither paper identifies the state of our 23 features or supplies an online normal/danger oracle. It is therefore unsafe to assume that all slow evolution is nuisance or that all rapid change is disruption.

## Six primary papers, with their actual access and training protocols

### 1. Learned self-supervision plus within-trajectory memory

Christos Ziakas and Alessandra Russo, **“VITA: Zero-Shot Value Functions via Test-Time Adaptation of Vision–Language Models,”** ICLR 2026. [Author project and conference citation](https://chziakas.github.io/vita/); [author manuscript, arXiv v5, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10085v5), §§3.1.1–3.2.1, Eqs. 1–3; §4.6.2 and Appendix F.2.

**Paper evidence.** A frozen CLIP encoder produces a representation `z_t`; the self-supervised loss reconstructs a learned projection of the same observation from another projection: `L_self = ||f_adapt(P_K z_t; θ_{t-1}) − P_V z_t||²`. The fast adapter takes a gradient step and its output, queried through `P_Q`, enters a regression head. The source states that the head “remains frozen” **at inference**. During meta-training the authors differentiate the supervised progress-prediction loss through the online steps, optimizing adapter initialization, projections **and the regression head**; normalized progress labels from expert demonstrations are used. At test time the current visual observation and goal description are available, and the adapter is updated before that observation's output; no target progress label is used in that update. The paper compares sequential carry with per-step-reset and full-trajectory alternatives in its own robotics benchmarks.

**Transfer boundary.** This is unusually close to a fixed-backbone/fixed-head *test-time* setup, but the head was co-trained with the adapter. It does not establish compatibility with our already-fixed risk head, per-shot reset, post-shot label schedule, or disruption alarms. A reconstruction-like auxiliary loss is learned to serve a task; its decline does not by itself imply every finite update improves that task. The paper's full-trajectory comparator is not a deployable causal baseline for early warnings.

### 2. Meta-training across a sequence of online distribution shifts

Chenyan Wu, Yimu Pan, Yandong Li, and James Z. Wang, **“Learning to Adapt to Online Streams with Distribution Shifts,”** arXiv:2303.01630v1, 2023. [Author full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.01630v1), §§III–IV, Algorithm 1, Eqs. 2–5, §§V-A and V-F. This citation is to the **2023 preprint version**; no later publication status is assumed.

**Paper evidence.** The inner loop processes a synthetic stream one sample at a time and updates shared representation parameters `ω` and auxiliary parameters `ϕ` using four-way image-rotation self-supervision; the task classifier `φ` is unchanged by the inner step. The outer objective applies supervised cross-entropy plus auxiliary loss to a separately sampled source-domain set after the stream updates, and meta-updates the initial shared encoder, auxiliary branch, and classifier. In testing, the current unlabeled image is used to update before its own prediction. Their stipulated test model has no raw past or future sample access, although the carried parameters encode past updates (§III, §IV-B). Tests include corrupted-image streams and video semantic segmentation.

**Transfer boundary.** This is not a learned-loss network: the image-rotation proxy is hand specified, while the initialization is meta-learned. It is also not an intra-shot reset design: the paper studies continuing domain streams. Moving the update from `ω` to an added Q/K state with the original Transformer frozen changes the available gradients. Rotation of images is not a justified EAST signal transformation; the project should reuse only the *sequential inner-loop/labelled outer-loop distinction*.

### 3. Meta-auxiliary learning for event-like scores, with full-video access

Zahidul Islam, Sujoy Paul, and Mrigank Rochan, **“Test-Time Adaptation for Video Highlight Detection Using Meta-Auxiliary Learning and Cross-Modality Hallucinations,”** WACV 2026, pp. 6893–6902. [Official CVF record and accepted PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/html/Islam_Test-Time_Adaptation_for_Video_Highlight_Detection_Using_Meta-Auxiliary_Learning_and_WACV_2026_paper.html); [author full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.04924v1), §§3.1–3.5, Eqs. 1–3, Algorithm 1, §4.3 Table 4.

**Paper evidence.** Two MSE auxiliary losses predict self-attended audio features from video and visual features from audio, respectively, with the relevant target branch detached. The inner update changes shared and auxiliary parameters; a ground-truth-highlight BCE on the adapted shared parameters drives the outer update of shared and primary parameters. Training begins from jointly trained primary/auxiliary weights. At test time the auxiliary loss adapts the network to the available test video without highlight labels, and the primary-only parameters are used for scoring. Table 4 explicitly separates joint training, joint plus TTA, meta-auxiliary without TTA, and meta-auxiliary plus TTA. For example, on TVSum, the reported top-5 mAP is 70.25 for meta-auxiliary without TTA and 71.05 with TTA; these are the paper's dataset results, not EAST results.

**Transfer boundary.** Their test object is an entire video, with clip-level self-attention and cross-modal features. The reported procedure is **not demonstrated prefix-causal**, so adapting on a whole EAST discharge before early prediction would leak future signals. Their shared Transformer-like parameters are adapted and the primary head is trained before testing; freezing our source for both outer and inner training is a different experiment. Audio/video cross-modal hallucination is an analogy, not a prescribed plasma loss. The no-update versus update ablation is the useful evidence design.

### 4. Accumulating experience across tasks is not unlabeled intra-shot learning

Chelsea Finn, Aravind Rajeswaran, Sham Kakade, and Sergey Levine, **“Online Meta-Learning,”** ICML 2019, *Proceedings of Machine Learning Research* 97, 1920–1930. [Official proceedings record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/finn19a.html); [paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/finn19a/finn19a.pdf), §§3–5, Eqs. 3–5 and Algorithm 1.

**Paper evidence.** Follow-the-Meta-Leader chooses a shared initialization by minimizing prior tasks' losses **after** each task-specific update. Its practical procedure samples previously observed tasks from a buffer and differentiates a validation loss through a local training update. The few-shot illustration appends observed `(x, y)` pairs to the current-task dataset; thus it uses current-task labels, unlike our running-shot rule. The stated logarithmic regret result assumes, among other conditions, strong convexity, smoothness including a Lipschitz Hessian, and a restricted step size; §5 explicitly discusses practical non-convex models separately.

**Transfer boundary.** The structural lesson is to preserve historical experience in a shared meta-parameter rather than to carry a single shot's temporary state into the next shot. For EAST, using **only ended shots** to update that shared object is our adaptation of the idea, not the paper's original current-task label protocol. Its regret theorem does not certify a frozen nonlinear Transformer, learned auxiliary loss, fixed alarm threshold, or finite-sample safety.

### 5. Ordinary H-mode variation can overlap with warning features

J. S. Geng, P. Li, Y. D. Li, F. Chen, Y. Q. Chu, et al., **“Spontaneous evolution of density peaking factor in TEM turbulence-dominated H-mode plasma on the EAST Tokamak,”** *Scientific Reports* 15, 7738 (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-91363-8. [Publisher full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91363-8); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11883010/). Locate “Experimental set-up and conditions,” Figs. 2–4, and “Spontaneous evolution of density peaking factor and TEM turbulence,” including Fig. 6.

**Paper evidence.** In the reported H-mode evolution of EAST shot 75261, after the L–H transition, density and the XUV radiation signal rise while core electron and ion temperatures gradually decrease, with LHW/NBI heating described as stable. During the selected 3.3–3.7 s interval, the authors report increasing density peaking and weakening measured turbulence; Fig. 6 examines five selected discharges. This is an observed operating **phase**, not a demonstration that the whole shot was labelled non-disruptive in our dataset. The authors discuss an associated confinement-improvement mechanism; the correlation and model interpretation should not be recast as a new intervention proof.

**Transfer boundary.** This shows that density, radiation and temperature can change together during H-mode evolution; those trends alone do not establish what will happen later. Because the final outcome of this example is not established here, it is not a labelled non-disruptive counterexample. Its diagnostic channels, operating regime and final outcomes are not verified to match our 23 features or labels. It supplies no recipe for automatically identifying a normal shot while it is running.

### 6. EAST density-limit evolution can end in disruption without the assumed precursor

Jiaxing Liu, Ping Zhu, Dominique Franck Escande, Wenbin Liu, Shiwei Xue, et al., **“Accessing the density-free regime with ECRH-assisted ohmic start-up on EAST,”** *Science Advances* 12(1), eadz3040 (2026), DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adz3040. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adz3040); [full text in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12757026/), Results → “Experimental results,” Fig. 1, Table 1, Fig. 2 and Materials and Methods → “Basic experimental conditions.”

**Paper evidence.** The authors vary ECRH power and prefilled gas in EAST density-limit experiments. For reference shot 143069, density follows a feedback-controlled ramp, then drops rapidly at the limit; the authors describe this case as occurring “without any prior manifestation of MHD activities.” Figure 1 records density, heating, current and bolometric radiation. The paper defines density limit using the maximum immediately before disruption and reports some other quantities averaged over 5–6 s close to disruption. It reports high-density operation under its particular conditions, not a universal safe threshold.

**Transfer boundary.** Do not equate this shot's ohmic/ECRH scenario with Geng's H-mode cases, or its bolometric total radiation with Geng's XUV signal. The 5–6 s averages and the density-limit definition are retrospective, and may not be used as live inputs before those times or the outcome arrives. Absence of a reported MHD precursor in this shot does not mean that no precursor exists in every shot, nor that density alone distinguishes outcomes.

## One falsifiable small experiment — proposal, not a paper result

**Question.** With the same learned auxiliary component and historical inter-shot policy, does *carrying its actual Q/K update within the current shot* improve fixed-threshold alarms over no update, and does real matured feedback contribute beyond a matched replacement? This is a controlled extension of the locally documented anchored-feedback setup, motivated by VITA's intra-trajectory comparison and Highlight-TTA's no-update ablation. It is not a reproduction of either paper, and is not a novelty claim.

1. **One mechanism and one training objective.** Reuse the existing bounded temporary Q/K coordinates `a` and, if available, the existing small auxiliary component `δ_φ`. For an origin `s`, let `x_s` be the then-available input, `u_s` the subsequently matured, valid feedback, and `z_a(x_s)` the frozen source representation with temporary state `a`. A candidate internal loss is

   `L_aux(a; s) = 0.5 || z_a(x_s) − stop(z_0(x_s)) − δ_φ(u_s, context_≤s) ||²`.

   On each eligible maturity, make one bounded step `a ← Π_A[a − η ∇_a L_aux]`; use the already specified Q/K radius and missing-value policy. `stop` here applies to the zero-state reference; `δ_φ` is constant with respect to `a` but must retain its outer gradient to `φ`. Backpropagation **through** the fixed source to `a` is necessary; the source weights and risk head remain frozen. If the existing component/loss is not actually available or has zero useful Jacobian in this state, stop rather than invent an implementation or assume an update occurred.

   After the first-20 seal/reveal boundary, fit `φ` using only those 20 completed shots by unrolling the same predict-then-maturity-update clock and minimizing a shot-normalized **post-update primary risk loss** on later windows within those completed shots. Their labels are used only after their shots ended. The loss is a surrogate, not the alarm criterion. Fix the horizon, inner step, radius, unroll length, data masks, optimizer budget, and model-selection rule before suffix replay. No premise says that lower auxiliary MSE or lower BCE guarantees better alarms.

2. **Intra first; fixed offline replay order.** For the primary comparison, use each shot in the current full360 stratified order exactly once. For ranks 1–20, preserve and count the same sealed source-only predictions in every arm; do not retrospectively rescore them with a target-fitted parent or intra adapter. Only after all 20 predictions are sealed and the 8D/12S outcomes revealed, fit the permitted first-20 parent and `φ_0` under the existing preparation contract, or reuse that exact parent only when its first-20 provenance and post-seal fit are verified. The J-TEXT source, risk head and alarm rule remain fixed. The resulting parent is common to all suffix arms; `φ_0` is frozen throughout the primary **ranks 21–360 / suffix340** intra comparison. Outcomes can be added to an inter buffer after their shot is sealed, but cannot update the primary comparison's shared policy. If the primary screen in step 4 does not pass, do not claim an intra benefit or advance the combined procedure.

   For each suffix shot, initialize `a=0`. At decision `t`, save the risk score/alarm using the current state; then release only feedback whose full observation horizon has matured and apply it for **later** predictions in that same shot. Never use the current-shot outcome, future within-shot values, future-derived normalization, or future masks to select an update. Seal all arms' predictions before revealing that shot's outcome. Earlier/later **shot ranks** refer to the stipulated offline replay, not wall-clock chronology.

   Only after an intra benefit is demonstrated, a separate exploratory inter-plus-intra replay may start again at rank 21 from the same prepared parent and `φ_0`, with a predeclared shared inter-update rule and only completed, previously revealed replay-rank outcomes. Never initialize that replay using outcomes from later suffix ranks. Its paired arms must use the same inter history and update schedule to isolate the intra contribution. Because full360 and this order have development exposure, neither stage is independent future validation.

3. **Matched arms.** On the same suffix340 shots, shared first-20 parent and source inputs in the fixed offline replay order, compare: (i) `a=0` throughout, (ii) real feedback with sequential carry, (iii) real feedback but resetting `a=0` at each maturity before the same one-step update, and (iv) sequential carry with a predeclared causal mean-feedback replacement from completed replay history. Prefix20 remains source-only and identical in all arms. Match the availability clock and masks across all arms, and the update budget, radius and learning rate across the three active arms; retain actual query inputs in all arms. Log valid-update counts, actual Q/K step norms and logit differences. Do not force equal nonzero norms when feedback naturally yields zero; report the mismatch. The carry-vs-reset comparison tests temporary memory; real-vs-mean is a specified intervention, not proof of a unique physical mechanism.

4. **Predeclare falsification.** Primary development screen: relative to no update, real-carry must produce at least one **net additional** timely detected disruptive shot in suffix340 (rescued minus harmed > 0) under the existing first-alarm rule and fixed threshold, create **no new normal-shot false alarm**, and meet the existing full360 FPR ≤ 0.20 contract. Report full360, source-only prefix20 and suffix340 counts/denominators separately; include the original prefix20 predictions in every full360 result. Report first-alarm lead times, including the locally documented strict `>30 ms` rule and long-lead cases, without redefining eligibility. Separately, for the feedback-use claim, predeclare the suffix340 shot-equal contrast `0.5 × [mean_normal(BCE_mean − BCE_real) + mean_timely-danger(BCE_mean − BCE_real)]` on later eligible within-shot windows; require it to be positive and report every shot and both group components. Labels enter these calculations only after each shot is over. A nonpositive contrast rejects this particular mean-replacement mechanism claim; it need not disprove all operational value. A failed operational screen blocks promotion even if MSE or BCE improves.

   For this exploratory screen, require at least five ended normal shots and five ended disruptive shots in suffix340, plus at least one shot in each group with two valid matched maturity updates and a later eligible query. Count all shots eligible under the existing alarm protocol in their respective full360 and suffix340 denominators, including those with no update; use the maturity requirement only to determine whether the memory mechanism was exercised. These minima make the comparison logically possible and give FPR a finite denominator, not statistical power or evidence of safety. If they are not met, report **inconclusive**, not success.

This is a development replay conditional on the first-20 fitted parent, not a safety demonstration. The current prefix is 8D/12S; the 17D/3S normal-sparsity warning in the older note belongs to its historical order. The current offline stratified order and full360 have development exposure; no suffix result is an independent or naturally future test. Multiple prior searches/choices and correlated windows invalidate a claim of independent confirmation. Only a genuinely unexposed cohort with the complete procedure locked in advance could provide an independent test. No run is authorized or performed by this note.
