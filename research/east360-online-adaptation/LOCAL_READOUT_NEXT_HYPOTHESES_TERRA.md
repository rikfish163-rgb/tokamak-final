# Local readout: why forecast adaptation need not identify a useful risk update

2026-10-29. Bounded, AI-assisted research memo; **development evidence only**, not a systematic review, a new physical discovery, or an independently validated result. The six papers below are the papers whose relevant methods were actually opened for this memo; several had already been read by the project, so this is not a claim of six newly discovered papers. No GPU experiment was run for this memo.

## Result to explain

The just-completed [five-arm run](probe_outputs/local_readout_east360_v1/complete.json) reports, on the same full 360 discharges, including cold20, and the fixed threshold 0.7258946895599365:

The true and reverse local arms both harm safe shot 64562; the hold-control arm also loses timely detection of 64835. These are the run's recorded comparisons; equal shot-level decisions do **not** establish identical risk curves, gradients, or mechanisms. Each updating arm recorded 10,774 nonzero updates. The report's necessary intra screen is false. The main agent owns the independent audit; this memo independently inspected the small completion and auxiliary summaries, not the audit's computations.

On 1,352 sealed future-signal samples from 340 shots, with each shot averaged equally, the mean SmoothL1 losses in source-asinh coordinates were: hold-last-value **0.02500649**; frozen readout before/after its neural update **0.08797539 / 0.08370189**; local true before/after **0.03463500 / 0.03448098**; local reverse after **0.03579139**; local hold-control after **0.02754575**. These are diagnostic forecast losses, not risk metrics. Local fitting improved forecast error relative to the frozen readout, but remained worse than plain hold, and no independently attributable intra risk gain was demonstrated. The local-before versus local-after difference is small; no claim of significance follows.

The relevant implementation is [learning.py](experiments/risk_aligned_online/local_readout_20261029/learning.py) and [run.py](experiments/risk_aligned_online/local_readout_20261029/run.py), against the [stream plan](LOCAL_READOUT_STREAM_PLAN.md). At a feedback clock the first two of four already matured, disjoint H25 targets are added to a per-shot readout fit; the newer two provide the neural gradient, then enter history. The cold encoder provides 64 last-signal-token features; each channel's 65-coefficient, 25-horizon readout is regularized toward the cold20 prior, with at most 16 historical rows. The readout itself does not alter risk: only the temporary, normalized first-block Q/K step can do that. The original model, risk head and alarm rule remain fixed; there is no post-cold20 inter-learning.

### Why this outcome is plausible, without claiming a unique cause

1. **Forecast loss and risk loss answer different questions.** Predicting common, easy-to-predict signal evolution can decrease average auxiliary error without distinguishing disruption from a safe but dynamically active trajectory. Mathematically, two conditional disruption outcomes can have the same distribution of the available future diagnostics given the same past; an auxiliary signal loss alone then cannot identify which outcome should receive a higher risk. This is an illustrative identifiability counterexample, not a finding that the EAST data actually satisfy that equality.
2. **Forecast values and update directions are different objects.** Let a fixed local readout be `A`, the last-token feature be `h(theta; past)`, and `r` its 25-step residual. Schematically the forecast gradient is `g_aux = J_h(theta)^T A^T psi(r)`, whereas a future labeled risk-loss gradient is `g_risk = d L_risk / d theta`. Improving the value of a forecast does not by itself ensure that its readout Jacobian, residual, or their projection into the Q/K risk-sensitive directions makes `g_aux · g_risk` positive. Worse, the risk loss is evaluated at a *later* window. A positive local first-order dot product is neither a finite-step guarantee nor a first-alarm guarantee.
3. **The fit is underdetermined without its prior.** A channel has at most 16 fit rows for 65 feature/intercept coefficients; the ridge solution is unique because of the cold prior, but data from this shot constrain only the observed design row-space. Different forecast readouts can fit the available observations similarly yet imply different derivatives outside the observed directions. This is a linear-algebra observation about this design, not proof that it caused the observed false alarm.
4. **The current target control changes two things.** `local_reverse` fits the older readout with reversed targets *and* reverses the newer gradient targets; `local_hold` likewise changes both. Thus the three diagonal arms cannot by themselves identify whether any difference comes from the fitted readout, the recent target, their interaction, or an update direction largely insensitive to either. Reverse is a synthetic control, not a physically valid alternate trajectory. The prior [forecast-head study](../Fuison/docs/research/source_event_rebuild_20261023/预测头校准_完整360与下一窗口基线_20261029.md) also found matching shot decisions across true/reverse/hold feedback; that does not mean the saved curves were identical.
5. **Context, censoring and lead time can hide or reverse the association.** Similar activity can have different trajectories or recorded outcomes; prior control interventions may also change the outcome. Conversely, a beneficial update cannot undo a first alarm that already happened. The current 23 processed channels and their upstream extraction/units are not fully certified, so neither a statistical channel name nor a high attribution proves a particular plasma instability.

The earlier [readout-identifiability report](../Fuison/docs/research/source_event_rebuild_20261023/冻结表示读出与跨炮泛化_源域及冷启动诊断_20261029.md) found conditional cold20 held-shot increment error 0.034219 versus hold 0.026810; its parent was already conditioned on all 20, and it was not an independent cross-device test. The earlier [attention-direction report](../Fuison/docs/research/source_event_rebuild_20261023/历史反馈权重与注意力更新方向_配对实验_20261029.md) found that learned channel weights and Q/K directions did not establish intra benefit; the [event/gradient study](../Fuison/docs/research/source_event_rebuild_20261023/事件与参照梯度_五组完整360复核_20261028.md) found true event teaching did not beat its controls. The [local slope study](../Fuison/docs/research/source_event_rebuild_20261023/炮内反馈失效_变化约束与突变放大实测_20261028.md) found amplification and worse aggregate forecasting. These are limitations of those specific tests, not impossibility results.

## Six primary papers checked, and exactly what transfers

Search on 2026-10-29 used web queries for test-time gradient alignment, online adaptation evaluation, cross-device scenario matching, risk-aware alarm scoring, and physics-based radiation dynamics. Inclusion: original method/experiment with accessible relevant methods; exclude abstract-only records from method claims. The selection is purposive and non-exhaustive; no PRISMA or broad coverage claim is made.

1. **Sun et al., 2020, “Test-Time Training with Self-Supervision for Generalization under Distribution Shifts,” ICML**, [author/publisher PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/sun20b/sun20b.pdf), §§2 and 4, especially Theorem 1. The model has a main and an auxiliary head on a shared feature extractor; the paper's sufficient condition relates a small auxiliary step to positive main/auxiliary gradient inner product. Its words are “gradient correlation as a determining factor” in the smooth convex setting. **Borrow:** inspect the direction of an actually available auxiliary update against the *later* main task, not just the auxiliary loss. **Limit:** the theorem assumes smooth, convex losses and a prescribed small step; image-rotation experiments are not a proof for this nonconvex, temporally delayed Q/K intervention. Do not infer that positive cosine necessarily improves first-alarm decisions.

2. **Du et al., “Adapting Auxiliary Losses Using Gradient Similarity,” arXiv v2**, [author PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02224), §§2–3 and 6. Their method uses “cosine similarity of gradients between tasks” to suppress an auxiliary contribution that opposes a supervised main-task gradient; it explicitly warns about noisy gradients and does not guarantee faster convergence. **Borrow:** separate genuine signal for the downstream task from an auxiliary update that merely optimizes itself. **Limit:** their deployment of the rule has access to the main-task gradient; current-shot EAST disruption labels are unavailable. Therefore any target-outcome gradient here is an offline diagnostic only, not a permissible online gate or policy. The project's earlier supervised-gradient weighting/subspace failures must not be renamed as this method.

3. **Zhao et al., 2023, “On Pitfalls of Test-Time Adaptation,” ICML**, [publisher PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zhao23d/zhao23d.pdf), §§3–4.3 and Algorithm 1. They distinguish episodic from state-carrying online adaptation and study model selection including a label-using oracle; their online experiments can suffer “over-adaptation on previous test batches.” **Borrow:** preserve the temporal access contract and report a label-informed oracle only as an oracle; fix comparisons and selection in advance. **Limit:** image classification and batch-to-batch parameter carryover are not this experiment's Q/K reset-at-each-feedback design. This paper does not show that current local-readout failure is caused by its particular batch-dependency mechanism.

4. **Zhu et al., 2021, “Scenario adaptive disruption prediction study for next generation burning-plasma tokamaks,” Nuclear Fusion**, [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.08956), §§2–3 and Table 2; published DOI [10.1088/1741-4326/ac28ae](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ac28ae). They partition C-Mod, DIII-D and EAST by flattop-averaged beta-p, input power and q95; they compare target-regime prediction with training drawn from differently matched regimes/devices. The authors describe “matching operational parameters of other existing machines.” **Borrow:** test whether an apparent dynamic/risk association survives comparable operating contexts, rather than assume an across-shot association transports. **Limit:** this is supervised cross-regime/cross-machine training and its split uses whole-flattop quantities. It is not an online causal selector, and our `qa_proxy`, `ne_nG` or Ip/Bt ordering must not be silently substituted for certified q95, beta-p or input power.

5. **Murari et al., 2024, “A control oriented strategy of disruption prediction to avoid the configuration collapse of tokamak reactors,” Nature Communications**, [publisher full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46242-7), Results “Characterising the plasma state” and Methods “Diagnostics of the radiation emission.” They distinguish configuration, kinetic-profile and radiation indicators; one MARFE example tracks a visible-camera emission centroid between frames, and other diagnostics use profiles and tomography. The paper explicitly says “Radiation anomalies appear also in discharges that in the end do not disrupt,” sometimes with intervention or fast termination. **Borrow:** activity, operating context and subsequent dangerous evolution are distinct; measure matched positive and safe trajectories where observable. **Limit:** those JET video/profile signals, control records and geometry are not reconstructed from our processed XUV/SXR statistics, and safe outcomes can be intervention-censored. No paper-specific pixel threshold or plasma-mode label is imported.

6. **Keith et al., 2024, “Risk-Aware Framework Development for Disruption Prediction: Alcator C-Mod and DIII-D Survival Analysis,” Journal of Fusion Energy**, [publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10894-024-00413-y), Models and Methods for Comparing Disruption Predictor Performance. They compare binary and survival models and “find the first time when an alarm is triggered”; a shot-level true positive requires enough warning, while a safe-shot alarm is an FP. They also model censoring in the survival formulation. **Borrow:** keep first alarm, strict lead time, class-separated shot outcomes and censoring interpretation distinct from per-window auxiliary or BCE metrics. **Limit:** their models are trained/tested on the same devices with substantial data, not J-TEXT→EAST intra adaptation; their tested horizons and hyperparameter selection do not authorize changing this project's fixed threshold or replacing its source classifier with survival regression.

**Source-check limitation.** Six candidate papers above had accessible original methods checked against their citations in this single agent's context. No independent fresh-context skeptic was available here; this is not an independent adversarial verification. One additional candidate, Rea et al., *Progress Toward Interpretable Machine Learning–Based Disruption Predictors Across Tokamaks*, was **not** counted as a methods-read paper: the publisher abstract was accessible but the MIT full-text fetch failed, so its detailed method was not used. No claim of a complete retraction/COI audit is made. The six included papers are three ML-method works and three tokamak works; this deliberate small sample is not a representative survey.

## Next experiment 1 — priority: factor the readout and the arrived target

**Hypothesis H1.** The real-target information may be lost or reversed at a specific interface: readout fit, recent-target residual, or Q/K-to-risk sensitivity. In particular, the equal shot outcomes of true and reverse controls do not identify which interface is responsible.

**Minimal diagnostic.** After the current run is sealed, replay the same signal-only prefixes at a predeclared fixed sample of at most four feedback clocks per eligible suffix shot (the same evenly spaced rule as the current auxiliary probe). Reconstruct history in chronological order, using the exact cold20 prior, masks and parent. At each clock independently fit `W_true`, `W_reverse`, and `W_hold` on the same older mature rows. For each *fixed* W compute equally normalized Q/K proposals from the same newer two mature windows using true, reversed or hold increments: a **3 × 3 readout-fit × gradient-target factorial**. Include frozen-prior/true and zero-update reference. This contains the existing diagonal constructions, while `W_true` with true versus reverse or hold isolates the newer targets without changing the fitted readout; the complementary comparison holds the target fixed while changing W. Preserve zero-gradient cases as zero, not artificially normalized directions. This requires a causal replay: the existing summary alone does not contain all required per-clock W and gradients.

At the first subsequent prediction point, and across the interval until the next feedback, compare the actual finite-step risk logits with zero update and the matched same-W controls. Only **after the full shot is sealed**, form `g_risk` and BCE diagnostics on safe windows and on disruptive windows whose actual lead is strictly `(30, 125] ms`; leave earlier disruptive windows unlabelled for this diagnostic. Separately record the earlier, already-triggered alarms and examine whether changes could affect the *first* alarm. Report per-shot aggregated effects, counts, availability masks, the actual finite-step changes, and the local first-order cosine/dot product where both norms are nonzero. Preserve all results, including 64562 and 64835; do not select a best clock or a channel from their outcomes. A shot-level first-alarm comparison for all new factorial arms requires replaying their complete causal curves, not stitching oracle-selected clock effects together.

At the same fixed sampled clocks where six disjoint mature blocks exist, also reconstruct the A/B/C split defined in H2. Fit on A and older non-overlapping history; compare the fitted readout with hold on B; compute true-C versus reversed/hold-C proposals with the same fitted W. After sealing only, stratify their finite risk effects and eligible-shot counts by the predeclared B qualification. This prospective-information diagnostic is what could justify H2; it must include the closed-gate group and both outcome classes, not just a favorable subgroup. It is still a retrospective analysis of development data, not confirmation.

**Refutation / decision.** Predefine the primary diagnostic as follows: at the fixed eligible clocks, average `BCE(true proposal) − BCE(control proposal)` over eligible subsequent points within each shot, then average those shot means separately for timely-disruptive and safe shots. For each stratum, report this paired quantity against both the same-W reversed and same-W hold proposal. A directional screen requires all four stratum/control means to be strictly below zero; if any is nonnegative, this hypothesized consistently favorable true-target direction is not supported. If a stratum has no eligible shots, the screen is inconclusive, not a pass or a failure. This sign screen is not a significance test, and the full 3 × 3 effects and counts remain visible. Conversely, if useful finite directions exist but fail to change first-alarm outcomes, timing/threshold margins rather than purely direction may be limiting. Neither result proves that every possible feedback objective fails. Formal significance or generalization is not claimed on the reused development flow.

**Information and physics boundary.** Online proposals use only arrived signal; later observations and outcomes are accessed solely for sealed retrospective evaluation and never to select a deployable clock, direction, gate, or step. `g_risk` is an outcome-informed oracle diagnostic, not an online update. No event labels or unverified plasma-mode semantics are required. Stratifying by a channel is descriptive only until its units, timing, missingness and physical meaning are separately established.

## Next experiment 2 — conditional and lower priority: disjoint reliability abstention

**Hypothesis H2.** Some past contexts may support a locally predictive readout whose *true-target* Q/K update is more risk-aligned than its control, while in other contexts updating is harmful. A three-way within-shot split might abstain where no predictive support is observable. This is a weak, high-risk engineering hypothesis, **not** an assertion that forecast accuracy implies risk accuracy. Do not run it merely because forecast loss decreases: first use H1 to check whether a predeclared, past-only qualification is associated with favorable finite risk-direction differences in both classes. If it is not, stop rather than promote this gate.

**Minimal candidate if H1 supports further testing.** At the existing 128-point feedback cadence, choose six chronological, pairwise target-disjoint H25 origins wholly contained in the observed prefix: older `A1,A2` for local ridge fit, middle `B1,B2` for qualification, and newer `C1,C2` for the Q/K gradient. With a 128-point input history, the earliest possible six-block opportunity is no earlier than the existing scheduled clock at 384; explicitly record lost early opportunities. Fit the prior-regularized W using A and older allowed history only; exclude any prior row whose target overlaps B or C. Use only channels active in the cold prior whose last observations and all 25 target points are present and finite in each of B1, B2, C1 and C2; A/history fit masks remain channel-specific. On this common channel set, score the frozen W on the two B target blocks and compare with holding each B-origin's last observed value, using the same SmoothL1 and masks. If the common set is empty, or W does not strictly beat hold, emit an exact zero Q/K state; otherwise calculate the same normalized 0.05 Q/K update from C on that same set. Append B/C to history only after the decision. No raw data copies, predicted-value feedback, extra classifier head, risk-score addition, alarm delay, or alarm threshold adjustment.

The comparison to hold is a **past-signal reliability check only**. It is neither a proxy outcome label nor evidence of danger; a gate can also suppress the very abrupt or rare evolution needed for warnings. This is not a rerun of mean-bias subtraction, `forecast_profile` residual-shape loss, or a joint source-classification/forecast training loss. Its distinct change is *abstention based on a disjoint past qualification block*, with all successful updates still made inside Q/K. It remains close to the previously unsuccessful forecast family and is worth running only if H1 reveals a credible risk-alignment subset; otherwise it should be abandoned.

**Controls and outcome.** At the identical six-block clocks and same gating decisions, compare gated true C feedback with gated reversed-C and gated hold-C feedback, gated frozen-readout/true-C, and zero-update cold. Include an ungated true-C arm using the same A/C data and clocks, to distinguish the effect of abstention from changing opportunity timing. Gate computation is common across the matched gated arms; reversed-C does not mean the entire B qualification was reversed. Keep Q/K update norm and all masks equal when nonzero, and record gate-open counts and coverage by shot, safe/disruptive class and after-seal lead group. The primary screen is the current one: full 360 including cold20, TP not lower than cold, FPR ≤ 0.20, and candidate J strictly higher than cold, gated reverse, gated hold, gated frozen, and the ungated comparator. Report every rescued/harmed shot and >3000-ms early alarms; auxiliary improvement, gate accuracy or a favorable late-window BCE is not a substitute.

**Refutation / boundary.** Reject H2 for this design if it fails the predeclared shot-level screen, if real C does not outperform the equally gated reversed/hold C controls, or if the gate almost always closes and merely reproduces cold. Do not retune the qualification rule using the 340 future outcomes, and do not claim that a successful gate learned physical causes. Compare intra alone first; only if the independently attributable intra screen passes should the main agent plan a separate fixed-contract inter/both interaction test. Since all 360 shots have been repeatedly development-exposed, even a passing result needs genuinely untouched future data before confirmation.

## Access and reproducibility caveat

Both proposed experiments preserve the qualified J-TEXT source model and risk head, the same cold20 operator and order, the 23-input contract, fixed alarm threshold, and strictly causal current-shot feedback. They introduce no J-TEXT online learning or post-cold20 inter-learning. They rely on actual timestamps and availability rather than assuming sample counts are milliseconds. The existing ordering based on initial Ip/Bt is an offline commissioning proxy, not verified damage; the 20-shot cold set contains 17 disruptive and 3 safe shots. Unknown upstream feature causality, physical units, missingness, device correspondence and control interventions remain material risks. A source-only method section, a deterministic numerical replay, and a full-360 development result are all different kinds of evidence.
