# Primary evidence: time scales and within-shot adaptation

Date: 2026-11-01. Bounded, AI-assisted public-literature sidecar; not a systematic review. No experiments or new data statistics were run. Project facts below are the user's supplied context unless explicitly identified as a source claim.

## Bottom line

Cross-tokamak temporal transfer is a legitimate **hypothesis to test**, not evidence that all EAST dynamics are five times slower than J-TEXT dynamics. Shen et al. selected the 25 ms and 125 ms disruption-label boundaries by a prediction-performance scan; they are not measurements of a common physical time-scale ratio. Separately, the user's project source-label rule is `max(25ms, AdvancedTime)`: 25 ms is the **minimum positive window** (floor), not a maximum, and the formula imposes no upper bound on `AdvancedTime`. Zheng et al. discuss a different quantity, resistive diffusion time, and find that their recurrent layers transferred less well than some early feature-extraction layers. Their layer comparison does not isolate time-scale mismatch from the other differences between machines. Windsor et al. tested a physics-motivated time normalization for **JET versus ASDEX Upgrade**, not J-TEXT versus EAST, and found sensitivity to the selected threshold and normalization. None of these studies demonstrates causal within-shot adaptation of this fixed model.

The cleanest immediate question is narrower: **does distributing the existing shot-local latent correction across past tokens according to already observed local change improve fixed-policy EAST alarms, compared with the current correction and a timing-control allocation?** This is an activity-conditioned weighting test, not a physical clock estimate or a time-warp implementation.

## Five primary papers

### 1. Shen and collaborators: the exact 25/125 ms labels are a model-selection choice

**Chengshuo Shen, Wei Zheng, Bihao Guo, Yonghua Ding, Dalong Chen, Xinkun Ai, Fengming Xue, Yu Zhong, Nengchao Wang, Biao Shen, Binjia Xiao, Zhongyong Chen, Yuan Pan and J-TEXT team (2024).** “Cross-tokamak disruption prediction based on domain adaptation.” *Nuclear Fusion* 64, 066036. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ad3e12); [author preprint, full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.05361). The preprint title is longer and its version may differ from the journal article.

- **Checked:** preprint §2.1–2.3, Table 1, §3.1 and Table 2, §4.2 and Fig. 5. In §3.1 (PDF p. 8), the authors say they selected thresholds by “finding the best performance of the model,” scanning 5–50 ms on J-TEXT and 5–500 ms on EAST and selecting 25 and 125 ms before current-quench onset. The 1 ms slicing cadence and those thresholds have separate roles. The paper excludes pre-threshold samples from disruptive shots rather than automatically calling them safe.
- **What transfers:** PGFE converts device-specific measurements into analogous engineered features; supervised CORAL (S-CORAL) aligns source and target feature covariances separately by class. The reported cross-device setup has 100 non-disruptive and 10 disruptive labeled EAST training shots; it is **offline supervised domain adaptation**, not unlabeled adaptation within a live shot. Its classifier is DART, not the fixed J-TEXT neural model here.
- **What does not follow:** a tuned ratio of label windows is not a physical scaling law, an alarm lead-time requirement, or a justified resampling factor. The user's source rule `max(25ms, AdvancedTime)` has a 25 ms minimum positive window and no implied upper bound on `AdvancedTime`; it is not assumed to be identical to this paper's constant-label implementation.
- **Raw inputs:** Table 1 and §2.1 show Mirnov arrays and radiation arrays; Mirnov processing uses windowed FFT and cross-spectral information. The preprint specifies 50 kHz and 10 kHz low-pass cutoffs for the J-TEXT and EAST Mirnov signals, respectively, before FFT. Reproducing those operations or changing their raw windows needs the original sufficiently sampled diagnostic waveforms, timing and channel geometry, not just 1 kHz feature summaries.

### 2. Zheng, Xue, Shen and collaborators: resistive time is one physical scale

**Wei Zheng, Fengming Xue, Zhongyong Chen, Dalong Chen, Bihao Guo, Chengshuo Shen and colleagues (2023).** “Disruption prediction for future tokamaks using parameter-based transfer learning.” *Communications Physics* 6, 181. [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-023-01296-9); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42005-023-01296-9).

- **Checked:** the publisher's HTML was directly retrieved for the `τ_R` definition in Results and discussions → “Training and evaluation of the source model on the J-TEXT tokamak.” The page explicitly calls `η` “the magnetic diffusivity.” Other claims below rely on indexed publisher full-text passages around Fig. 6/Tables 2–3, Fig. 1, and Methods “Labeling” and diagnostic selection; the complete figures and supplement were not independently checked.
- The article writes the illustrative resistive diffusion scale as `τ_R = a²/η`, with `a` minor radius and `η` **magnetic diffusivity** (SI units m²/s), not electrical resistivity in Ω·m. In the usual scalar SI convention, writing electrical resistivity as `ρ_e` gives `η = ρ_e/μ₀` and thus `τ_R = μ₀a²/ρ_e`; the two uses of the symbol must not be interchanged. It reports approximate typical values of 25 ms for J-TEXT and at least 500 ms for EAST. Those are paper-level estimates for a particular physical process and conditions; they are neither measured for these 23-feature shots nor a universal multiplier for every instability, feature, or alarm. In particular, they are **not** the 25/125 ms label ratio.
- The authors compare freezing choices after pretraining on J-TEXT and fine-tuning with 20 labeled EAST shots. Freezing the ParallelConv1D feature layers and adapting the LSTM/classifier performed better in their comparison than also freezing the LSTM. They interpret this partly in terms of tokamak-dependent longer time scales; architecture, operating scenario, diagnostics and labeled target training also changed the inference. This is not causal proof that time scale alone caused the difference.
- Their Fig. 1 uses several diagnostic sampling rates (including high-rate Mirnov and density inputs) within 1 ms feature frames. Their own fixed-label choices, 62 ms and 172 ms, belong to **this different model/study**. Methods report a performance-based scan and discuss the alternative of precursor-dependent labels; the label values themselves are not direct physical-time measurements.
- **Availability:** `a²/η` cannot be inferred just from a feature name. The listed 23-feature schema does not expose the temperature and other inputs/calibration needed to estimate shot-specific magnetic diffusivity; actual access and units would have to be established separately. The high-rate feature extractor likewise requires original diagnostics, not the 23 summaries alone.

### 3. Windsor and collaborators: a cross-machine time normalization, with constraints

**C. G. Windsor, G. Pautasso, C. Tichmann, R. J. Buttery, T. C. Hender, the ASDEX Upgrade team and JET EFDA contributors (2005).** “A cross-tokamak neural network disruption predictor for the JET and ASDEX Upgrade tokamaks.” *Nuclear Fusion* 45, 337–350. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/0029-5515/45/5/004); [EUROfusion author manuscript](https://scipub.euro-fusion.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/EFDP04063.pdf); [author's full-text HTML](https://colin-windsor.github.io/HomePage/05disr/index.htm).

- **Checked:** HTML §§2–3 and 6–9, Eq. 1, Figs. 5 and 8 and conclusions. The target in Eq. 1 is `exp[-F (t_dis − t)/<t_con>]`, with a device-average confinement time in the denominator. Their inter-device time normalization was motivated by an approximate minor-radius-squared resistive argument, giving about four for JET/ASDEX Upgrade. This is a normalization of the model's target/time-type quantities, **not blanket resampling of every raw diagnostic waveform**.
- The authors scanned the cross-machine ratio and also considered a separately optimized target threshold. Their §6 explicitly notes that target-device test information is needed for some optimizations; their conclusions say the optimum changes when the threshold is optimized separately. The study itself warns that input normalization and scenario comparability matter.
- **Transfer boundary:** the formula, devices, seven inputs and offline cross-machine experiments do not establish a J-TEXT/EAST ratio or a causal shot-local algorithm. A faithful physical reproduction would require the relevant confinement-time and equilibrium quantities/definitions; the 23-feature list includes proxies such as `qa_proxy`, not a certified replacement for every paper input.

### 4. Zhu and collaborators: scenario mismatch is an alternative explanation

**J. Zhu, C. Rea, R. S. Granetz, E. S. Marmar, K. J. Montes, R. Sweeney, R. A. Tinguely, D. L. Chen, B. Shen, B. J. Xiao, D. Humphreys, J. Barr and O. Meneghini (2021).** “Scenario adaptive disruption prediction study for next generation burning-plasma tokamaks.” *Nuclear Fusion* 61, 114005. [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ac28ae); [full author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.08956).

- **Checked:** §§2–3, Tables 1–2, Figs. 1–3, and the EAST-as-target appendix. The study compares low- and high-performance regimes on C-Mod, DIII-D and EAST, and examines the impact of matching operational parameters, including `q95` and `βp`, when transferring training data across devices. Its evaluation uses a 50 ms warning criterion motivated by the contemplated ITER mitigation requirement (§2), not a measured universal plasma time constant.
- **Relevance:** change in operating regime, feature distribution and mechanism can degrade transfer even within one machine. Thus a difference in per-shot latent activity or record duration is not uniquely evidence of a time-scale shift. Matching scenarios can improve the paper's offline cross-device setup, but this is not proof that the present frozen model learned dimensionless physics or that online temporal alignment will help it.
- **Availability:** `qa_proxy` and other available engineered channels may serve as descriptive, mask-aware proxies, but should not be equated to the paper's full `q95`, `βp`, power and other inputs without validated definitions and units. Its exact analysis may need equilibrium and power diagnostics absent from the 23 summaries.

### 5. Dixon: a genuinely online alignment algorithm, not fusion evidence

**Simon Dixon (2005).** “An On-Line Time Warping Algorithm for Tracking Musical Performances.” *Proceedings of IJCAI 2005*. [Official proceedings PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/post-0005.pdf).

- **Checked:** §1, the dynamic-programming recurrence and Fig. 1 algorithm, plus §2's music evaluation. At stream time `t`, online time warping aligns the observed prefix of one sequence to an initial subsequence of a reference. A bounded adaptive search band and run-length restriction limit computation and consecutive horizontal/vertical steps. The incoming target's future is not required, although a reference performance is available and alignment can be imperfect.
- **Transfer boundary:** this is music-to-music alignment with a corresponding reference, not neural test-time training, plasma dynamics or a validated tokamak alarm. An EAST shot need not be a speed-changed copy of any J-TEXT shot; reference choice, different event chains, mismatch, latency and early-path lock-in could dominate. Any eventual adaptation would have to use fixed source references only, restrict target access to the arrived prefix, handle missing features and validate no retrospective alarm revision. Do not apply offline full-shot DTW to a live-shot claim.
- **Current use:** motivation for a *future* bounded causal-alignment baseline if a temporal structure signal first survives the simpler test below. The proposed activity gate is **not** Dixon's algorithm.

## Separate the clocks and information losses

| Quantity | Meaning here | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Physical characteristic time | E.g. the paper's resistive `a²/η`, or a specific precursor's growth, rotation, or transport time; may depend on conditions and mechanism. | A single constant applies to every feature or shot. |
| Sampling/output cadence | Both project streams are stated to be 1 kHz: adjacent samples are 1 ms apart. | Equal physical dynamics, equal bandwidth of the underlying instruments, or equal upstream filtering. |
| W128/P8/H25 | At 1 kHz, 128 samples nominally cover 128 ms (first-to-last timestamps span 127 ms); P8 is eight 1 ms samples per token; H25 is the stated 25-step/25 ms auxiliary horizon. | An intrinsic plasma time scale. H25 is distinct in purpose from the source disruption-label rule `max(25ms, AdvancedTime)`, whose minimum positive window is 25 ms. |
| Feature extraction / averaging | A windowed FFT, cross-spectrum, array moment, or patch mean is an operator with its own input rate, window, alignment and latency. | That the extracted 1 kHz stream preserves the raw waveform, or that a label boundary is an extraction window. |
| Label horizon and operational warning | The source rule `max(25ms, AdvancedTime)` gives a 25 ms floor, without an implied upper bound on `AdvancedTime`; the EAST label setting is 125 ms. These define supervision; a mitigation lead-time rule defines what counts as a useful alarm. | The onset time of every precursor, a common physical time ratio, or that label and warning rules must be identical. |
| Record duration | The supplied typical source record is about 346 ms and a safe EAST shot about 5 s. | A physical dynamics ratio. The source duration is about 2.7 **non-overlapping** W128 windows, not a count of all overlapping prediction endpoints. |

At 1 kHz, the time series **of a stored feature value** can describe changes up to its own Nyquist limit subject to its upstream processing. A stored `Mir_avg_fre` value can nevertheless summarize a higher-frequency mode estimated earlier from raw data; this does not recover the original high-rate waveform, phase, array geometry or a new FFT made with a different original window. Similarly, summary moments such as `sxr_mean`, `sxr_var`, `sxr_skew`, `sxr_kurt` and their XUV counterparts do not generally invert to the full spatial array. The local feature-order listing was inspected, not the shot data or actual units; it explicitly does not certify units. Use missingness masks, and do not assume `mode_number_n` or `MNM` is an ordinary continuous variable.

## Most falsifiable small test — proposal only

No training, replay, GPU job, threshold change, or data edit was performed here. This activity-gate sketch is a proposal, contingent on the approved data split and existing implementation contract. As user-reported coordination context, the main history sidecar found no executed input time-dilation test and suggests a separate `d1` versus `d2` comparison with an early source-baseline fallback. A CPU raw-variogram/TR1353+ended20 audit is running; its results have not been inspected or duplicated here.

1. **Freeze the contract.** Hold the qualified 23-feature J-TEXT model, source normalization, W128/P8/H25, existing risk head, threshold and alarm policy fixed. Reset every shot-local state at the shot boundary. Compare on the same predeclared EAST development shots, with a later held-out evaluation only if the development gate passes. Do not use EAST outcomes, future samples or final-shot statistics to update the model while a shot is running.
2. **Make one observable gate.** At endpoint `t`, compute complete, mask-aware eight-sample patch means in the *existing* source-normalized coordinates. For each pair of successive arrived patches in the current window, define activity as the median absolute change across continuous, jointly available features; exclude `mode_number_n` and other channels whose continuity is not established. Require at least three valid channels for a patch-pair activity, otherwise leave its gate at one. Relative to the median of eligible activities in that **current prefix/window**, clip the ratio to `[0.5, 2]`; if the median is zero/nonfinite or there are fewer than two eligible pairs, use all-one gates. These constants define one falsifiable candidate, not a measured physical prior.
3. **Reallocate, do not learn a clock.** Apply the gate only to the already-existing per-token latent correction at the same insertion site. For each endpoint, multiply gated corrections by a common factor so that their summed squared latent norm equals that of the original corrections over valid tokens; if either norm is zero/nonfinite, fall back to the original correction. Compare (A) the current correction, (B) activity-gated correction and (C) the same gate values deterministically rotated among eligible past tokens, with the same energy matching. All values assigned at endpoint `t` must already be available by `t`; never revise previously emitted predictions. This controls correction energy, though it does not guarantee identical logits or all other effects.
4. **Decide on alarms, not just forecast loss.** On paired shots report fixed-threshold, fixed-policy disruption TPR at the project's existing operational lead-time criterion, safe-shot FPR, first-alarm/lead-time distributions, and paired changed-shot counts; also report auxiliary forecast loss separately and the fraction of endpoints/shot-time using nontrivial gates versus fallback. Do not substitute the 125 ms training label for the operational warning requirement or recalibrate the threshold on the evaluated shots. Small-shot uncertainty must be shown, not treated as a decisive physical law.
5. **Falsification / abstention.** The proposed mechanism fails this screen if, with adequate nontrivial gating, B does not improve the prespecified useful-alarm comparison against A and C, or only improves auxiliary prediction while alarms do not improve. If eligible activity or nonzero corrections are too rare, report the test as **uninformative**, not a negative physical result. A promising development result still requires an untouched held-out shot-level check and causal-prefix audit; it would establish a model intervention effect, not a new law of plasma physics or a cross-device time ratio.

## Search and verification limits

Public searches covered Shen/Zheng J-TEXT–EAST domain adaptation and time scales; JET–ASDEX time-normalized prediction; other groups' cross-device regime matching; and causal online time warping. Selection favored primary papers with equations or methods/protocols available, and includes a counter-explanation to a pure time-scale story. This is a bounded shortlist, not an exhaustive search or novelty assessment. Four retained papers had their author/proceedings full text available. For Zheng et al. (2023), the `τ_R` notation was later checked directly in the publisher HTML; the other cited claims remain checked against indexed publisher passages, not a complete review of figures or supplement. No source was human-read or independently verified by a separate agent; the requested no-agent restriction was followed.

**Correction record:** The earlier sidecar incorrectly described 25 ms as a maximum for the project source label. At the user's correction, it is now recorded literally as `max(25ms, AdvancedTime)`, with 25 ms a floor. The distinction between the project's rule and Shen et al.'s scanned constant labels remains, as does the conclusion that neither establishes a fivefold physical warp.
