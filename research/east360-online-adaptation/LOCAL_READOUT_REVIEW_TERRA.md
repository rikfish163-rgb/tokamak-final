# Independent causal and design review: local readout

2026-10-29. Scope: read-only review and small CPU probes; no GPU jobs or changes to experiment code/data. The five-arm run and all data are development-exposed.

## Verdict

I found **no demonstrated P0 implementation bug** in target maturation, masks, state reset, or the cold comparator. The completed experiment does **not** establish an intra-shot risk benefit under this implementation: on full360, cold and frozen-readout each have 125 TP/32 FP; local and reverse each have 125/33; hold has 124/33. The local arm harms safe shot 64562 versus cold. The declared screen fails. I independently recounted these decision records from `probe_outputs/local_readout_east360_v1/per_shot.json`; `complete.json:5-224` reports the same. This is a result about the deployed fixed-step design, not proof that all intra-shot learning or the fitted signal mapping is useless.

The main independent audit reports 360-decision/auxiliary recount, cold20 NumPy-coefficient maximum error 8.19e-14, and exact risk/auxiliary replay on five shots; its replay uses the production feature forward and is not a full independent neural replay (`probe_outputs/local_readout_east360_v1/audit.json`, `replay`). I inspected that result but did not rerun or reimplement the audit.

## Material finding 1: fixed step confounds decoder quality with optimization geometry

**Research/design limitation, not P0.** Each feedback uses `delta = -0.05*g/sqrt(||g||^2+1e-16)` from a zero Q/K state, with no loss-decrease acceptance (`experiments/risk_aligned_online/local_readout_20261029/learning.py:168-188`). A small gradient therefore generally receives essentially the same full step norm as a much larger gradient. That is an equal-norm direction comparison, but it is not an equal optimization-quality comparison: a local fit that reduces the residual can make a fixed step too long for the local loss curvature. The 0.05 step is not guaranteed to improve even the already-observed feedback loss.

The run records strict feedback-loss decreases in 6,325/10,774 local opportunities versus 10,774/10,774 frozen and 3,818/10,774 hold (`learning.py:271-280`; `audit.json`, `feedback_totals`). Thus 4,449 local cases were **non-decreases**, including any exact ties. Local mean feedback loss still declined overall; the counts alone do not prove that every non-decrease is an overshoot or that this explains the risk failure.

I tested the geometry on CPU using the actual parent, cold20 readout, input map, observed prefixes, and the production feature forward. Examples were deliberately selected for non-decrease, not sampled to estimate prevalence. On shot **73405**, clock **256**, true recent targets and masks were identical for frozen and local:

- Local gradient norm was 0.003245 versus frozen 0.082991; both proposed norm 0.05. Local feedback loss was 0.000904636 at zero Q/K, 0.000881166 at one-quarter of its actual proposal, and 0.000958447 at the full proposal.
- Along the local proposal, the first-order change `g·delta` was -0.000162245; the observed full-step change was +0.000053811. The positive nonlinear remainder was +0.000216056, larger than the predicted decrease. This demonstrates overshoot for that observed-loss example, not a prospective risk improvement.
- The exact linear decoder Jacobian with respect to its 64 unstandardized latent features is `coef[:64]/scale` (`learning.py:29-34`). Over all 21 active channels and 25 horizons, its Frobenius norm was 33.173 for the prior and 33.100 for the local fit; the difference's norm was 1.934. These are decoder Jacobians, not the full Q/K-to-prediction Jacobian. They do **not** support an assertion that the fitted decoder's overall Jacobian simply exploded. Its orientation, the encoder Jacobian, residual size, and directional curvature all matter.

Two further selected CPU examples support the same narrow mechanism: shot 63273 clock 256 had local loss 0.000893526 at zero, 0.000880979 at quarter-step, and 0.000972367 at full-step; shot 73541 clock 384 had 0.003184907, 0.003120528, and 0.003288627 respectively. Their frozen losses decreased at the full step. These diagnostics do not establish how a modified procedure would perform on future auxiliary targets or alarms.

The readout fit is an unweighted per-row sum of squared residuals with unit isotropic precision, not a posterior covariance learned from cold20; the prior itself was a shot-weighted ridge fit (`learning.py:79-105`; `readout_identifiability_20261029/learning.py:25-51,142-157`). At most 16 rows constrain 65 coefficients per channel. This is a deliberately specified update, but its scale is consequential: across the recorded 10,774 feedbacks the local coefficient-change Frobenius norm had minimum/median/maximum 0.437/2.781/23.707, against prior coefficient norm 14.554 including intercepts. Those coefficient norms do not by themselves diagnose output or Q/K Jacobian norms. The active cold scales ranged 0.06085–1.16013, so there is no evidence here of a near-zero-standard-deviation explosion.

If a follow-up is authorized, a **separately specified** bounded backtracking/accept-or-zero rule on the same already-observed feedback, or a decoder-smoothness constraint, could test this confound. Fix its finite budget and rule in advance, apply it to every relevant arm, report resulting step norms and feedback acceptance, and assess sealed future auxiliary and risk outcomes separately. Different accepted norms change the estimand, so retain an equal-norm comparison as appropriate. This is not a repeated learning-rate hypergrid, threshold tuning, or a retroactive rescue of this result.

## Material finding 2: the negative controls do not isolate history-target information

**Attribution limitation, not P0.** `local_reverse` and `local_hold` change both the older targets used to fit the readout and the newer targets used for the neural gradient (`learning.py:90-105,151-167`). Therefore local versus either placebo cannot independently attribute a difference to the earlier fitted mapping, as opposed to the newer feedback target or their interaction. By contrast, local versus frozen uses the same true recent gradient targets and masks, so it tests replacing the fixed prior by the local fitted head **within this normalized-step procedure**. It does not separate improved fit from altered decoder slopes, gradient direction, or step curvature.

For a future factorial attribution test, retain true recent feedback across all arms and change only the already-observed history-to-target association in a matched fit control; keep cold/frozen controls and fixed outcomes. Any such test remains developmental. It is not needed to conclude that this run failed its declared screen.

## Causal, mask, and comparator checks

- **Mature H25 split:** with count 256, origins are 155, 180, 205, 230. Older fit targets are indices 156–205; newer gradient targets are 206–255. The proposal first affects prediction index 256. Later iterations insert the newer rows only after feedback, and the deque is per-shot and capped at 16 (`deepcoral_prefix_20261022/learning.py:92-106`; `local_readout_20261029/learning.py:56-76,129-150,195-210,243-269`). Context windows can legitimately contain earlier targets; the separation is between target blocks, not independent histories.
- **Masks:** active prior channel, finite/available origin, and all 25 finite/available targets are required per fitted or gradient channel. Missing entries are excluded from the solve/loss; hold's zero targets are an explicit control, not imputation (`learning.py:37-50,87-100,133-143,161-180`). `fit_counts_max` is only a maximum over channels, not a per-channel support record (`learning.py:204-207`).
- **State and baseline:** `predict` creates fresh histories and zero states per shot; each feedback computes every Q/K proposal from zero, not from a preceding shot or previous Q/K proposal. After cold20, all arms share the frozen cold20 slow operator and input map; the first 20 are identical original-source predictions. Each cold curve is checked against the earlier sealed reference including threshold decisions (`learning.py:213-217,265-269`; `run.py:201-225,269-290,318-320`). The frozen arm is an auxiliary-feedback intervention, not a no-update baseline; `cold` is the no-intra comparator.
- **Auxiliary diagnostics:** the current fitted head is evaluated with zero Q/K for `*_before` and the newly proposed Q/K for `*_after` (`learning.py:281-305`). This is a cold-parent counterfactual, not necessarily the immediately preceding deployed Q/K state. The four sample clocks are chosen using eventual shot length, so their selection is retrospective even though the sampled forward uses only the prefix; it does not affect the adaptation. Outcome-dependent terminal validity is applied only after sealing (`run.py:38-58,226-268`). Partial future masks in this diagnostic differ from the complete-channel feedback/fit mask, so its numbers are not directly interchangeable with the earlier readout OOF scores.

The auxiliary after-update means were hold 0.025006, frozen 0.083702, local 0.034481, reverse 0.035791, and local-hold control 0.027546 (`auxiliary_summary.json`). These are auxiliary errors on the diagnostic mask, not alarm gains; local is worse than simple hold here.
