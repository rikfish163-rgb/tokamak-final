# EAST intra-shot adaptation: normal coverage and useful update directions

2026-10-31. Targeted literature sidecar for the parent research process; **four primary papers, not a systematic or exhaustive review and not a novelty claim**. No GPU, training, model forward, project-code change, or project-data change was performed for this note.

## Decision in brief

The papers support distinguishing three questions: (1) does the learned auxiliary gradient improve the **primary task** after the update; (2) is there enough independent support to estimate normal and disruptive performance; and (3) does the *real feedback* add value over matched controls? They do **not** establish a universal requirement that real feedback must beat each corrupted-feedback control within every group. Such a condition is a legitimate, conservative, predeclared **mechanism screen**, but is neither equivalent to an operational safety constraint nor logically necessary for overall paired operational benefit.

Do not retroactively change the existing screen: its safe-group real-versus-reverse/donor criteria failed. The alternative estimand below is a proposed future analysis, not a reason to call the current experiment passed. In particular, the normal evidence consists of only **three shots**, and a group-balanced loss or many overlapping windows cannot create additional independent normal shots.

## Four primary papers: methods, assumptions, and transfer limits

### 1. Hatem, Qian & Wang — Point-TTA, ICCV 2023 [1]

**Method.** Their network has a shared geometric encoder, primary point-cloud-registration branch and auxiliary branch. Three auxiliary losses are reconstruction, BYOL-style feature learning, and a correspondence task using known synthetic transformations; their scalar weights are learned. They first jointly train the primary and auxiliary objectives. They then meta-train: for a training point-cloud pair, form adapted parameters `φ = θ − α∇θ L_aux`, evaluate the **labeled primary registration loss after adaptation**, and differentiate it back to update `θ` (Eqs. 7–9, Algorithm 1). The same pair appears in their displayed inner and outer objectives; this is not evidence for a shot-disjoint support/query split. The paper's motivating warning is that auxiliary updates may “improve the auxiliary task and not the primary task” (§3.3).

**What is trained or frozen.** Unlike this project, Point-TTA jointly trains and meta-trains the main shared model and primary branch before deployment. At test time it adapts model parameters to each point-cloud pair with the auxiliary loss, then uses the adapted shared/primary branches for registration; §4.1 says five gradient updates in training and testing. The text describes updating all parameters, whereas `L_aux` in Eqs. 6–8 is written as a function of shared and auxiliary parameters, not primary-only parameters. Thus the equations alone do not establish a nonzero primary-only inner gradient; this sidecar did not inspect the implementation. It is not a frozen-backbone/auxiliary-only experiment.

**Evidence and limit.** Training uses labeled transformations on 3DMatch, evaluation includes 1,623 test scans across eight indoor scenes and an outdoor transfer dataset. Table 6 reports DGR + meta-auxiliary **without** test-time adaptation at 92.28 registration recall versus 92.45 with the full method: substantial benefit can come from meta-training rather than the online update itself. This is a valuable matched ablation, not a statistical guarantee for every pair. It does not show that an auxiliary-only 14k head, one Q/K step, three normal shots, or plasma alarms benefit.

**Transferable test.** Optimize and evaluate the post-update primary risk, not signal-forecast improvement alone; separate a frozen/no-update model, meta-trained-but-no-update, real feedback, and matched controls. Infer local gradient alignment only as a diagnostic; in the actual EAST design the primary labels used for such diagnosis must remain offline and shot-held.

### 2. Wang, Duan, Fang, Suo & Gao — imbalanced sequential domains, ICCV 2021 [2]

**Method.** This is continual **meta-training**, not their own unlabeled test-time auxiliary-gradient method. Tasks arrive in imbalanced latent domains; the paper uses MMD-based change detection, a replay memory chosen using estimated domain prevalence and task/cluster gradient-norm difficulty, and adaptive memory task sampling (M2D3 and PETS, §§2–3.4). Their variance derivation uses importance-weighted task gradients: the ideal sampling probability is proportional to the task distribution times its gradient norm (Eqs. 10–12); the practical cluster approximation is proportional to `n_i G_i` (Eq. 14). This is not simply equal group sampling or a theorem that large gradients are causally useful. The displayed high-level PETS pseudocode does not itself spell out the inverse-probability factor from the theorem, so do not transfer the theorem to a different sampler by assertion.

**Sample and parameter assumptions.** Their six image domains provide a long sequential task stream; task labels and domain boundaries are unavailable, but each few-shot classification task has labeled support and query examples. They evaluate 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot recognition with ANIL and prototypical networks, without a pretrained feature extractor. Base §2 assumes consecutive domains without revisiting, with revisiting examined separately. Here a “shot” in 1-shot image classification means one labeled support **image per class**, not one EAST plasma discharge. The method jointly trains its feature/meta-learner using new and replayed tasks; it is not a fixed-source Transformer plus an auxiliary-only sidecar.

**Transferable test and limit.** Track independent discharge identity and effective number of normal shots in each fit, not just class-balanced window counts. The current fixed cold20 data are already jointly available, so adding the paper's online domain detector or replay system is not implied. Equal shot weighting or a group-robust objective can test the *idea* of coverage, but cannot recover absent operating regimes; gradient norm is also sensitive to scale and outliers.

### 3. Ren, Zeng, Yang & Urtasun — learning to reweight examples, ICML 2018 [3]

**Method.** For a supervised training minibatch, start example perturbation weights `ε_i` at zero, take a hypothetical one-step weighted parameter update, then differentiate a **clean labeled validation loss** through that step with respect to `ε_i`. Rectify negative weights to zero, normalize the nonnegative weights, and perform the actual weighted training update (§3.1, Eqs. 5–9, Algorithm 1). Equation 12 makes the relevant first-order quantity a dot product between a training-example gradient and validation gradient. Thus the informative object is alignment with an explicitly chosen target distribution, not simply a large auxiliary gradient.

**Sample and parameter assumptions.** This is supervised model training with a small, clean, representative validation set, not unlabeled test-time adaptation. In the binary imbalanced MNIST experiment the paper splits a **total of 10 balanced validation images** from 5,000 training images; the CIFAR noise experiments use 1,000 clean images or 10 per class in a different setup (§§4.1–4.2). It updates the supervised classifier, not just a separate auxiliary head. Its smoothness/bounded-gradient/full-validation-set convergence analysis (§3.4) concerns the stated optimization conditions and empirical validation loss, not out-of-sample safety or the validity of labels for a three-shot normal population.

**Transferable test and limit.** On allowed, completed-shot training episodes, compare each actual signal-gradient direction with the later primary-risk gradient for each group, and then measure the **actual finite step** on held-shot queries. This is an EAST diagnostic inspired by the paper, not the paper's algorithm. Historical labels can define the offline query; they must not be used to choose the update on the current live shot. Three normal shots do not establish the paper's representative-validation assumption. Dot-product sign is first-order and can disagree with a finite normalized Q/K update.

### 4. Sagawa, Koh, Hashimoto & Liang — group DRO, ICLR 2020 [4]

**Method.** Define groups using observed training group labels and minimize the maximum empirical **absolute group loss**, not real-minus-corrupted performance (§2, Eq. 4). Training group identity is assumed known; test group identity is not needed by the predictor. On overparameterized ResNet50/BERT models, both ERM and group DRO can fit all training groups yet fail on the worst test group (§3.1). Stronger regularization or early stopping helped group DRO on their tested benchmarks (§3.2), though stronger L2 did not help their BERT/MultiNLI setting. Their optional `C/√n_g` group adjustment is a heuristic with a tuned constant, not a calibrated safety bound for dependent windows (§3.3).

**What changes and limits.** They fine-tune full pretrained models on image/text groups, with smallest training groups of 56, 1,387 and 1,521 examples across their benchmarks; group validation is used for model selection. These are not comparable counts of independent EAST shots. They do not study a frozen-source H25 auxiliary head, disruption timing, feedback corruption, or whether every group must prefer real to corrupted feedback. Their own §4 shows that equal group upweighting is a different objective and can hurt. In particular, `max_g ΔBCE_g` or a max over risk-plus-alignment terms would be a **new project-specific surrogate**, not their algorithm or a hard no-harm guarantee.

**Transferable test.** Compare actual per-shot normal and timely-danger risks and their fit/held gaps under a fixed training budget; if a group-robust outer objective is explored, isolate that objective from all other changes and report the sacrificed group as well as the worst group. With 2 normal shots per fit fold, any apparent robust improvement is especially vulnerable to memorizing those identities.

## Is real > corrupted on *each* group scientifically necessary?

**No, not for the general claim of useful operation.** A real-feedback procedure could have better prespecified paired operational utility than cold and controls overall while a corrupted control happens to perform better on normal shots. Conversely, beating both controls on every group's mean ΔBCE would not ensure acceptable first-alarm TPR/FPR, adequate warning time, or even no harmful individual normal shot. A direct absolute safety condition against the cold baseline, stated in deployment units, addresses a different question from a real-versus-corrupted superiority condition.

The stronger per-group condition **can** be defensible if the intended claim is that aligned feedback provides added value in *each group*, if worst-group protection is itself the predeclared scientific question, or if a stakeholder explicitly requires it as a conservative promotion rule. It can also reject a useful overall system when subgroup estimates are noisy or when a corruption happens to induce a helpful generic update. With three normal shots, report individual paired differences and uncertainty/inconclusiveness rather than treating a group mean or leave-one-shot-out range as a precision guarantee. A mean that does not worsen is not a no-harm guarantee either.

Reverse and donor are imperfect interventions: reversal can preserve some level/trend information and donor feedback can share structure or induce a beneficial generic direction. Real-versus-control differences test the specified interventions under matched conditions; they do not on their own prove a unique physical mechanism. Include cold/zero-update and, if already available, a norm- and clock-matched generic-update control. Do not declare physical-law learning from either feedback ordering or lower auxiliary error.

## Actionable next test for the parent — proposal, not an implementation

1. **Lock the estimands before seeing new outcomes.** First, paired operational utility: same fixed threshold and first-alarm protocol, report timely TPR, normal FPR, rescued/harmed shot IDs, and alarms with lead >3,000 ms; use the existing strict lead >30 ms and FPR ≤0.20 contract. Choose in advance how to trade a TPR change against FPR, whether the control comparison is a superiority or descriptive analysis, and an absolute normal-harm tolerance. Do not silently replace the previous failure gate or call the already exposed EAST360 stream independent confirmation.
2. **One controlled coverage ablation, conditional on identifiable distinct objectives.** Hold the fixed parent, 14k auxiliary architecture, data, one-step Q/K update, H25 timing, initialization, optimizer budget and sampled episodes fixed. Compare the existing outer objective with **one** predeclared shot-normalized, group-aware alternative, e.g. a mean primary-risk term plus a fixed penalty for positive normal-group mean ΔBCE. This asymmetric safety penalty is not group DRO. Form group means by averaging within a shot and then across shots; train only on the allowed ended shots and keep labels out of online feedback. If the existing objective already equals the proposed one, do not present it as a new ablation. The penalty weight and training tolerance must be selected before the comparison; the penalty is a surrogate, not an operational guarantee. Do not add normal data, change the backbone or start an experiment under this sidecar's authority.
3. **Match controls and clocks.** For each fixed candidate, compare cold/zero-update, real, reverse and donor on identical shots and eligible queries with the same source inputs, feedback availability, update budget and magnitude policy. Prespecify how validity masks travel with reversed/donor values, and use matched common support where feasible; never invent values for missing channels. Save the current prediction before releasing feedback; affect only later scores, reset temporary state at each shot, and never use the current shot's outcome to choose or tune an update. For retrospective direction analysis, compare `∇a L_signal · ∇a L_risk` at the same state with the observed finite-step risk change; compute it using labels only after the corresponding prediction is sealed.
4. **Report coverage rather than manufacture it.** For each arm show normal and danger per-shot ΔBCE against cold, paired real-minus-control differences, early-normal behavior, fit versus held group means, and all normal shot identities. Show equal-group and any operationally motivated weighting separately; they answer different questions. The existing shot-held folds are development-only because the parent has seen all 20, and 2–3 normal shots are inadequate for a tight safety conclusion. A genuinely unexposed, later shot cohort and a locked protocol are needed for an independent assessment; any future data access or run requires the parent to establish its authorization.
